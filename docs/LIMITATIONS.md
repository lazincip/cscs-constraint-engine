# Known Limitations (`v2026.10.2`)

Published, not hidden. Each limitation is stated with its measured evidence.

1. **Friction-scale compression & wall permissiveness at small seed counts.** With 4 enrolled seeds, hashed-embedding friction compresses into a narrow band: fluent text of almost any topic scores 0.53–0.66. The calibrated wall ($\tau_{95}$, the 95th percentile of leave-one-out *seed* friction) therefore sits above most fluent far-OOD prompts: a fresh v2026.10.2 engine admits **29/30** fluent far-OOD probes (median friction 0.5835 vs. wall 0.6566), while still ranking in-domain below far-OOD on average (friction AUROC 0.83–0.92 on the committed battery; **0.8056** on an auditor-written held-out set). The gate is a **trajectory governor and telemetry source, not a hard out-of-domain security boundary** at small seed counts. Deployments needing stricter admission should enroll more seeds (≥ 10 recommended), run `learn_manifold=False` (frozen mode), or both. Isolation probes confirm this permissiveness is a baseline calibration property, not session drift: 300-turn sessions change far-OOD admissions by zero (see [BENCHMARKS.md](BENCHMARKS.md)).
2. **Cold-start residual geometry.** The centered SVD residual of *any* fluent prompt is floored near $\sqrt{1 - k/512} \approx 0.988$ at seed scale, because a handful of seeds span almost none of the 512-dimensional hash space. Raw-residual separation margins are therefore intrinsically small (seed-scale margins 0.02–0.06); the former $\ge 0.10$ residual-margin criterion was **retired in v2026.10.2 as geometrically unreachable**, and evaluation now uses friction separation and AUROC with the raw residual reported alongside.
3. **Stateful near-neighbor / far-OOD closeness.** In stateful mode the committed battery's near-neighbor median friction ($C = 0.6061$) marginally exceeds its far-OOD median ($D = 0.6007$): strict $A < C < D$ ordering does not hold in stateful mode (it holds in frozen mode). The $C$/$D$ gap (0.0054) is within cross-environment variance; treat the two families as one "outside the core" band rather than a ranked pair.
4. **Content policy is out of scope.** The quality gate rejects malformed, repetitive, keyword-stuffed, and spam text, but an instruction-override or policy-violating request written in fluent grammatical English (battery item `E3`) is *admitted* — correctly, for a trajectory governor: it is lexically and topically well-formed. Content policy belongs to the operator's layer, not this engine. In particular, the gate does **not** detect instruction-override ("jailbreak") text — `E3` scores quality 1.0 — so do not rely on this engine as an injection defense.
5. **Unspaced scripts (CJK) are out of scope.** The quality gate tokenizes on whitespace, so unspaced Japanese/Chinese text scores as a single token (quality 0.20) and is blocked. This is a documented scope limit of the tokenizer, not a judgment on the content; CJK support requires a segmenter and is scoped separately.
6. **Verifier scope.** `DriftVerifier` scores *specificity density and drift*, not truth. A reply that echoes the prompt with a wrong specific can score VERIFIED, and a correct reply dense with names/years can score AMBIGUOUS/SPORADIC — the signal is stylistic, and verdicts should be consumed as telemetry, not fact-checks. In dream mode the verifier now scores replies (capped at 0.75) instead of returning a constant; a sufficiently assertive fabrication can still reach SANCTIONED_DREAM by design when `dreaming=True` — sanctioned dreaming is an operator-invoked mode, and its outputs must be labeled as such downstream.
7. **Lyapunov exponent scope.** The reported Lyapunov exponent is negative *by construction* (the contraction map's step sizes decay as $\Phi^{-t}$); it evidences the iteration's convergence, not an empirical property of the dialogue. It is retained as an internal stability diagnostic.
8. **Cross-platform BLAS/LAPACK variance.** Sign canonicalization keeps cross-machine friction agreement tight (≤ 0.005 on most turns), but near-degenerate singular values can shift intermediate coordinates across CPU architectures and BLAS backends. Evaluate **relative within-run ordering** rather than raw cross-machine scalar equality.
9. **State restore.** `T3LBStateCodec.decode()` parses a serialized seed; reconstructing a live engine state from a seed (`load_seed`) is not implemented in this release. The seed is a telemetry/summary artifact, not a checkpoint format.
10. **Latency is environment-dependent.** v2026.10.2 performs additional anchor passes per turn and measured 2.6 ms median / 3.4 ms P95 over 300 distinct-prompt turns on the reference VM (Linux, Python 3.12.3, numpy 1.26.4); v2026.10.1 measured 0.45–1.07 ms medians on other hardware. Long prompts (~2,000 words) reach the ~10 ms class. No cross-machine latency guarantee is made.
11. **Gradual ("boiling-frog") drift.** The two-zone commit budget bounds state mutation *per turn*, but a session can still walk out of the domain in small, individually admissible steps. In a blind-audit probe (Meta AI, 2026-11-07; independently reproduced), a five-step lexical ramp from the seed topic to an unrelated topic was allowed at every step (friction 0.46–0.62, never approaching the wall), and the early steps committed state — each step is judged on its own, so no single verdict sees the ramp. There is **no cumulative drift budget** in this release. Deployments that fear slow ramps should monitor the friction *trajectory* (reported per turn) rather than any single verdict; a cumulative budget is scoped for a future round.
